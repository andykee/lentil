Controlling the output plane#

propagate_dft() provides different ways for controlling the exact region of the output plane that is computed. This can significantly improve propagation performance in some circumstances.

shape

Size of the output plane. The returned arrays are shape * oversample.

prop_shape

Size of the region that is computed, centered on the optical axis. Defaults to shape.

shift

Moves the optical axis, and the computed region with it, within the output plane.

mask

Restricts computation to the bounding box of the nonzero values in mask.

Everything outside the computed region is zero. A Wavefront stores only the computed data and the field and intensity attributes place it in a shape array when they are read.

prop_shape#

prop_shape sets the size of the computed region in the output. Decreasing prop_shape improves performance, but data outside the region is lost:

For most pupil to image propagations, a prop_shape of 128 or 256 pixels balances speed and coverage. For image to pupil propagations, the computed region must contain the whole re-imaged pupil, so it is safest to leave prop_shape at its default.

prop_shape pays off most when modeling large tilts. See Propagation with large tilts.

shift#

shift moves the optical axis by a number of output pixels. It is given in native pixels and is scaled by oversample internally.

# move the optical axis +20 px in x and +10 px in y
w = lentil.propagate_dft(w, pixelscale=5e-6, shape=128, shift=(20, 10))

Shifts from fitted tilt are added to shift.

mask#

mask restricts the computation to part of the output plane, such as a detector subarray or within a field stop. It must have shape shape * oversample. Only the rectangular bounding box around its nonzero values is computed, and the mask values are not applied to the result.

# only compute the region covered by an off-axis 40 x 60 px subarray
mask = np.zeros((256, 256))
mask[150:190, 160:220] = 1

w = lentil.propagate_dft(w, pixelscale=5e-6, shape=256, mask=mask)
